Lifeboat

Android prototype · Solana mainnet

Lifeboat is an emergency Android client for Solana positions. Withdraw, repay or claim when a protocol's website or backend is unavailable but the Solana blockchain still works.

The app reads positions directly from the chain and builds transactions itself. Supported actions need only a working RPC endpoint and your external wallet; they do not depend on protocol websites, APIs or indexers.

Recover funds from your positions

  • ORE: claim recorded SOL and ORE mining rewards, withdraw available staked ORE, and claim staking rewards while keeping the staked balance. Pending mining SOL that has not been recorded is not settled by Lifeboat.
  • Kamino Lending: repay debt and withdraw collateral from supported main-market positions, including Multiply and eMode. Repayment uses funds already in your wallet; Lifeboat does not swap collateral or use flash loans to unwind a position.
  • Meteora DLMM: withdraw all liquidity from a selected supported position, claim its fees and rewards, and close the empty position account. You receive the original tokens; WSOL stays wrapped. Large withdrawals may need several transactions, with saved progress for recovery.

Review and sign on your phone

Lifeboat simulates each transaction and shows the expected amounts and fees before approval. Your external wallet keeps your private keys and signs the transaction; Lifeboat submits it through the RPC endpoint.

If a submission result is uncertain, check its saved signature before starting another operation. An interrupted Meteora withdrawal can resume after the pending result is resolved.

What you need

Android 7 or later, an external wallet compatible with Mobile Wallet Adapter, and access to Solana mainnet. The current build uses the public Solana RPC endpoint and needs no API key.

This is a prototype under device testing. Support depends on the position, token and wallet; review the amounts, recipient and simulation result before signing. The guides below describe the supported cases and current limits.
